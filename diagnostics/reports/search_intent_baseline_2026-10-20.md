# Search-Intent Baseline — Phase A of the search-intent test-set plan

**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Script:** `diagnostics/diag_search_intent_baseline.py`
**Raw data:** `diagnostics/search_intent_baseline_results.csv` (fixture battery only)
**Models under test:** `nomic-embed-text:latest`, `embeddinggemma:latest`, `embeddinggemma-2:latest`
(real Ollama `/api/embed`), plus the model-independent BM25 lexical tier.
**Status:** READ-ONLY. Nothing in the codebase or any database was changed.

**Purpose:** a baseline for the four semantic gates (`explicit_search_action`, `lookup_request`,
`research_intent`, `episodic_relevance`) before the search-intent test set is rebuilt. It measures
each threshold source a gate can resolve to:

- hand-validated (nomic only);
- calibrated (re-derived with `_youden_calibrate()`);
- lexical (BM25);
- whichever of these production actually uses.

## Method

- **Battery:** the current fixtures, via `_build_gate_phrase_pools()`, scored two ways:
  - **raw** — phrases embedded as written, which is what calibration does;
  - **lowered** — phrases lowercased first, which is what production does. `Planner` scores the
    lowercased instruction; templates are embedded as written in both.
- **Pool sizes:** `explicit_search_action` and `lookup_request` share 3 positives (the
  explicit-search gate has none of its own) against 17 negatives. `research_intent` has 10 / 17 and
  `episodic_relevance` 13 / 20.
- **Real requests:** the user's 55 distinct past chat requests from the live DB.
  - The 23 generated by UI buttons (Live Feed "Tell me more…", release-notes summaries) are excluded:
    they carry pinned context and don't depend on the gates.
  - The 32 typed requests were labelled by Claude and reviewed by the user: 6 should search, 2 of
    them also research, 0 need memory.
  - Requests and labels are personal data; they stayed in the session scratchpad. Only aggregates
    and generic descriptions are recorded here.
  - "Search fires" means the explicit-search or lookup gate fired (both trigger `web_search`), with
    and without the deterministic keyword and explicit-date rules.
  - Other routes that can still produce a search (model-initiated tool calls, slash commands) are
    outside this measurement.

## Findings

1. **Calibration and production score different text.**
   - Calibration embeds battery phrases as written, but production scores the lowercased request.
   - nomic is unaffected: the average shift is 0.000.
   - Both EmbeddingGemma models are case-sensitive (average shift about 0.01). That is enough to
     raise false positives once the calibrated threshold meets lowercased text: `embeddinggemma-2`
     episodic 30% → 45%, research 6% → 29%; `embeddinggemma` research 12% → 24%.
   - A universal fix: calibrate on lowercased phrases, exactly as production scores them.
2. **The battery can't measure the two search gates.**
   - There are 3 near-duplicate "Can you look up …" positives, borrowed by the explicit-search gate.
   - nomic's hand-validated explicit-search threshold (0.68) fires on none of them.
   - For both EmbeddingGemma models the hard negatives (for example "would you look at this?")
     outscore every positive.
   - The lexical tier's perfect explicit-search score is in-sample on those same 3 phrases.
3. **The templates don't match how the user phrases searches.** A threshold can't fix this.
   - The lookup templates are polite questions ("can you look up the release date for this"). Real
     searches were short imperatives: "look up <topic> next", "search GitHub for <thing>",
     "research <topics>", "what can you tell me about <specific named model>".
   - Under nomic those scored 0.47–0.59 on the lookup and explicit-search gates.
   - "Look at this image" scored **0.77** on lookup, because "look at" sits close to "look up".
   - With a false alarm above every miss, no threshold separates them. The templates themselves need
     revising.
4. **No gate catches a bare "research <topic>".** `research_intent` only upgrades a search that
   already fired, and neither search gate recognises that phrasing.
5. **The BM25 lexical tier is length-biased.**
   - BM25 scores are unbounded and grow with input length and common words.
   - Its thresholds (about 2.6–3.4) were tuned on short phrases.
   - Long pasted messages (a transcript, a set of build notes) scored 12–16 and fired both search
     gates. Most of its 8 real false alarms were long or conversational messages, not intent.
   - Since the lexical tier is the deployed source for both EmbeddingGemma models' search gates, this
     is where their real-request false alarms come from.
6. **`embeddinggemma-2`'s episodic gate is loose in practice:** it fired on 15 of 32 typed requests,
   none of which needed memory. That is consistent with finding 1.

## Implications for Phase B

- Calibrate on lowercased text (finding 1). This is cheap and universal, and every model benefits.
- The new battery needs explicit-search positives of its own, and short imperative forms for lookup
  and research. It also needs hard negatives taken from the real false alarms: "look at this
  image", file and image questions, long pasted text, statements, follow-ups.
- **A battery alone won't fix findings 3 and 4.** The templates also need revising. To avoid
  overfitting, the revisions should be checked on a held-out part of the battery and on the real
  requests, not on the same phrases they were tuned on. This widens Phase B's scope: needs the
  user's go-ahead.
- The lexical tier needs a length-robust scoring or threshold (finding 5), recalibrated on the new
  battery.
